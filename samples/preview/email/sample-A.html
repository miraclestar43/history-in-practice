陈平投汉，范增求去：项羽留不住的两个人

汉之三年，荥阳被围，汉王用四万斤金买一条离间计。同一场战争里，项羽身边一先一后走了两个人，走的理由并不相同。

一桌好菜，一条缝

汉之三年，项羽几番截断汉军甬道，荥阳城里的汉王断了粮，派人请和，条件是把荥阳以西割给汉。项羽本想答应，历阳侯范增拦住：「漢易與耳，今釋弗取，後必悔之。」——汉军眼下好打，放过了要后悔。楚军随即急围荥阳。

照《史记》的写法，汉王这一次没有硬拼，而是想办法让楚营自己生疑，出主意的是早先从项羽那边投奔过来的陈平。项羽身边先后少了两个人：先是陈平投汉，后是亚父范增求去。两人都说过「骸骨」二字，走的理由却不一样。

时间、地点、人物

时间是汉之三年（一般折算为公元前204年），地点在荥阳，汉军被围、粮道被断。项羽身边有被尊为“亚父”的历阳侯范增，还有钟离眛、龙且、周殷等将领——按陈平的说法，这样的骨干「不過數人耳」。汉王那边，陈平此前已从楚营投来，这时任护军中尉，监护诸将。

三次转身

陈平投汉的路上就差点送命。渡河时，船家见他一表人才、独自赶路，猜他是逃亡的将领、腰里藏着金玉，起了杀心。他干脆脱掉衣服，赤身帮着撑船——「解衣躶而佐刺船」——船家看清他身上什么也没有，才作罢。

到了汉营，麻烦换了形式。绛侯、灌婴等人在汉王面前说他坏话：长得好看，像帽子上的玉，里头未必有东西；在家时「盜其嫂」；在魏、在楚都待不住；如今管护军，还收将领们的钱，「金多者得善處，金少者得惡處」。汉王召他来责问，陈平答得很直白：魏王不用他的计，他才去投项王；项王「不能信人，其所任愛，非諸項即妻之昆弟」，有奇士也用不上，他才离开。至于收钱，「臣躶身來，不受金無以爲資」。他最后说，计策若没用，钱都还在，可以封好交公，「得請骸骨」——放他走。汉王于是道歉、厚赐，拜他为护军中尉，监护诸将；诸将不敢再说。

陈平看楚营，看到的是一条缝：项王「意忌信讒」，真正撑得住的骨干不过几人。他请汉王拿出几万斤金行反间。汉王给了黄金四万斤，「恣所爲，不問其出入」——随他怎么花，不过问去向。钱撒进楚军，流言跟着起来：钟离眛等人替项王打仗，「功多矣，然而終不得裂地而王」，要和汉联手灭项氏、分地称王。项羽果然对钟离眛等人起了疑心。

最后是那一桌菜。项王派使者到汉营，汉王备下牛羊猪俱全的太牢，端上来，一见来人便装作吃惊：「吾以爲亞父使，乃項王使！」好菜撤下，换上粗劣的饭食。使者回去照实禀报，项王于是大疑亚父。范增主张急攻荥阳，项王不肯听。范增听说自己被疑，怒道：「天下事大定矣，君王自爲之！願請骸骨歸！」《项羽本纪》说项王「許之」。他还没走到彭城，背上发疽而死。

为什么会这样

两个人离开，都绕着同一个字：信。陈平说项王的信任只给项家人和妻族；范增是老臣，一旦被疑，连急攻荥阳的主张也没人听。陈平对项王还有一段更细的分析：项王「恭敬愛人」，所以「士之廉節好禮者多歸之」；可是「至於行功爵邑，重之」，舍不得封赏，于是另一些人「亦以此不附」。一种解释是（作者推断）：反间计咬得住，是因为流言说的正是“功多而不得封王”，踩中了楚营里本就存在的不满。至于项羽为什么就这样信了，史书没有细讲。

还有一处值得留意（作者提出）：陈平和范增都说过“请骸骨”。陈平说「得請骸骨」，是把去留交给汉王判断，结果被留下、被重用；范增说「願請骸骨歸」，是在被疑之后负气求去，项王也就放他走了。同样几个字，一次用来换取信任，一次说在信任破裂之后。至于太牢与粗食那一幕，情节太像戏，可能实有其事，也可能经过叙事加工，现有材料无法判断。

今天可以怎么用

一个小组里传出话：某位骨干拿了别家的邀约，快要走了。负责人第一反应是去查谁在传话。更有用的第一个问题可能是：如果这话是真的，她为什么会想走？传言有人信，往往是因为它说中了一件大家心里都知道、却没人摆上桌面的事，比如一拖再拖的晋升。先处理那件事，传言就没那么有力了。

一个外包转正的同事接手采购，老同事私下嘀咕他会让熟人的供应商沾光。他没赌咒发誓，只把三家比价的记录发进群里，每笔差价写明理由，谁都能翻。外来者要化掉怀疑，最省力的不是表态，是把过程摊开让人看。

别照搬的地方

汉王把四万斤金交出去、连去向都不过问，放在今天是内控事故，不是气度；陈平用这笔钱买的是对手的猜疑，不是自己人的协作，别把离间当成管理术。这些材料实际上只有一条史源，太牢一幕尤其戏剧化。还有，范增被疑之后求去，是史书记下的结局；被疑之后留下来把话说清楚，或另起炉灶，都只是作者提出的可能，并不是史书记载的范增当时的考虑。

想一想

虚构：六个人的咖啡店后场团队，年底分红由店长一人定，方案照入职年限排。一位从别家跳槽来的烘豆师改进了拼配，出品口碑最好，却分得最少；店里已有传言，说店长只照顾老员工。

这位烘豆师应该留下来把分配规则谈清楚，还是另找一家店？留下意味着当那个挑刺的人，未必改得动，还可能被贴标签；离开干净利落，却等于默认这套规矩，也放弃了已经积累的手感和熟客。

先停十秒，想想你会怎么做。

一个参考思路

假设：假设他手里有硬数据（近两个月各人排班、出品与差评记录），店长并非恶意，只是惯性加怕麻烦；假设本地还有两三家店可以去。

可以怎么做：先把排班与出品记录整理成一张表，只列事实；约店长单独谈一次，只谈规则口径，不谈人身；给出下次分红可采用的透明算法和一个过渡方案；若店长连列数据都不接受，再谈去留。

另一种合理选择：另一种合理选择是直接走人，去一家分配有成文规则的店。理由是信任一旦塌了，重建的成本全由个人承担，而离开本身也是给店方和同行的信号。两种都合理，取决于他怎么判断这家店还有没有得改。

出处与不确定性

全部材料出自司马迁《史记·陈丞相世家》《项羽本纪》以及班固《汉书》据《史记》的改写（A-E10 与 A-E4 同源），没有真正独立的第二史源；「太牢具—恶草具」一幕高度戏剧化，无从核对；「范增因反间而去」是《史记》给出的因果叙述（疑心→不听其计→求去→道中病死），不是独立证据；范增求去的原话两篇措辞不同（《陈丞相世家》作「願請骸骨歸」，《项羽本纪》作「願賜骸骨歸卒伍」）；陈平陈述的离魏、离楚理由出自他对汉王的自述，可能带有自我辩护成分（作者推断）；「盗嫂」「受诸将金」只是汉营诸将的指控，无独立核实；项羽、范增的内心活动原文未载，本文未写。“汉之三年”折算为公元前204年依通行纪年，不出自本篇证据。

Chen Ping Defects, Fan Zeng Walks Away: Two Men Xiang Yu Could Not Keep

In the third year of Han, Xingyang was under siege, and the King of Han spent forty thousand catties of gold on one plan to split the enemy apart. In the same war, two men left Xiang Yu's side, one after the other, for very different reasons.

A Fine Spread, and a Crack

In the third year of Han, Xiang Yu cut the Han army's supply road several times. Trapped in Xingyang and out of food, the King of Han sent an offer to make peace, on condition that everything west of Xingyang be given to Han. Xiang Yu was ready to accept, but Fan Zeng of Liyang stopped him: "The Han army is easy to deal with; if you let them go now and do not take them, you will regret it later." The Chu forces then pressed the siege of Xingyang hard.

As the Records tell it, this time the King of Han did not try to fight his way out. He looked for a way to make the Chu camp turn on itself, and the plan came from Chen Ping, who had earlier crossed over from Xiang Yu's side. Xiang Yu lost two men in turn: first Chen Ping, who went over to Han, and later Fan Zeng, his "Second Father", who asked to go home. Both men used the same old phrase for resigning, asking for their "bones", but they left for different reasons.

Time, Place, People

The time is the third year of Han (usually dated to 204 BC); the place is Xingyang, where the Han army was besieged and its supply road cut. Around Xiang Yu stood Fan Zeng, Lord of Liyang, whom he honoured as "Second Father", and generals such as Zhongli Mo, Long Ju and Zhou Yin. By Chen Ping's count, men of that calibre "were no more than a few". On the Han side, Chen Ping had defected from Chu some time earlier and now served as Commandant Protector of the Army, overseeing the generals.

Three Turns

Chen Ping nearly died on his way to Han. Crossing a river alone, he looked to the boatman like a runaway officer with gold and jade hidden in his belt, and the man eyed him with murder in mind. Chen Ping took off his clothes and helped pole the boat. Once the boatman saw he had nothing on him, he left him alone.

In the Han camp the danger took another form. The Marquis of Jiang, Guan Ying and others denounced him to the King of Han: handsome, like the jade on a cap, but perhaps hollow inside; at home he had had an affair with his sister-in-law; he had failed to settle with Wei or with Chu; and now, in charge of supervising the army, he took money from the generals, so that those who paid more got good posts and those who paid less got bad ones. The King of Han summoned him to explain. Chen Ping's answer was blunt. The King of Wei would not use his advice, so he went to Xiang Yu. Xiang Yu "cannot trust people; those he relies on and loves are all Xiangs or his wife's brothers", so even a man of rare talent was wasted, and he left. As for the money: "I came here naked. Without taking gold I had nothing to work with." If his plans were useless, he said, the gold was still there to be sealed and handed in, and he could ask for his bones, that is, be allowed to go. The King of Han apologised, rewarded him richly and made him Commandant Protector over all the generals. The generals said no more.

Looking at the Chu camp, Chen Ping saw a crack: Xiang Yu was "jealous and suspicious, and believed slander", and the men who truly held his side together were few. He asked for tens of thousands of catties of gold to sow discord. The King of Han handed over forty thousand catties, "to use as he pleased, without asking where it went". The money went into the Chu army, and so did a rumour: Zhongli Mo and others had won great merit for Xiang Yu "yet would never be given land to rule as kings", so they meant to join Han, destroy the Xiang clan and divide its lands. Xiang Yu did indeed begin to doubt Zhongli Mo and the others.

Then came the meal. When Xiang Yu sent an envoy to the Han camp, the King of Han had a full feast of beef, mutton and pork brought in. Then, on seeing the man, he pretended to be startled: "I thought you were the Second Father's envoy, but you are Xiang Yu's!" The fine dishes were carried out and coarse food served instead. The envoy reported all of it, and Xiang Yu came to suspect the Second Father deeply. Fan Zeng urged a quick assault on Xingyang; Xiang Yu would not listen. Hearing that he was suspected, Fan Zeng said in anger: "The affairs of the realm are largely settled. Let Your Majesty manage them yourself! I ask for my bones, to go home!" The Basic Annals of Xiang Yu record that Xiang Yu "granted it". Before Fan Zeng reached Pengcheng, an abscess broke out on his back and he died.

Why It Happened

Both departures turn on one word: trust. Chen Ping said Xiang Yu's trust went only to his own clan and his wife's family; Fan Zeng was a senior adviser, yet once suspected, even his advice to storm Xingyang was ignored. Chen Ping had also given a finer reading of Xiang Yu: he was "respectful and kind to people", so "men of integrity who valued courtesy flocked to him"; but "when it came to granting ranks and fiefs for merit, he held on to them", so others "for this reason did not stay attached". One explanation, and it is the author's inference, is that the plot worked because the rumour named a real grievance: great merit, no land. Why Xiang Yu believed it so readily, the histories do not say.

One more thing worth noticing, and this is the author's observation: both men used the phrase "ask for my bones". Chen Ping said it to put the decision in the king's hands, and he was kept and promoted. Fan Zeng said it in anger after he had been suspected, and Xiang Yu let him go. The same words, once to earn trust and once after trust was gone. As for the scene of the feast and the coarse food, it reads like theatre; it may have happened as told or been polished in the telling, and the surviving material cannot settle which.

How to Use This Today

Word goes round a small team that a key member has an offer elsewhere and is about to leave. The manager's first impulse is to find out who started the talk. A more useful first question may be: if it were true, why would she want to go? A rumour spreads when it names something everyone already knows but nobody has raised, such as a promotion that keeps getting postponed. Deal with that, and the rumour loses most of its power.

A colleague who moved from contractor to permanent staff takes over purchasing, and older hands mutter privately that he will favour suppliers he knows. He does not swear oaths; he just posts the three-way price comparison into the group chat and gives a reason for every price difference, open for anyone to check. For an outsider to dissolve suspicion, the cheapest move is not to make statements but to lay the process open for people to see.

Where Not to Copy

The King of Han handing over forty thousand catties of gold without asking where it went would be an internal-control failure today, not magnanimity. What Chen Ping bought with it was the enemy's suspicion, not cooperation on his own side; this is not a management technique. In practice all of this rests on one historical source, and the feast scene is especially dramatic. Finally, Fan Zeng asking to leave is how the histories record the ending. Staying to talk it through, or starting over elsewhere, are possibilities the author raises, not choices Fan Zeng is recorded as weighing.

Think about it

Fictional: a six-person back-of-house team in a coffee shop splits the year-end profit share, and the shop manager decides it alone, on a scheme ranked by length of service. A roaster who joined from another shop improved the blend and gets the best feedback, yet receives the least; there is already talk in the shop that the manager only looks after the old hands.

Should the roaster stay and get the rules of the split talked through, or look for another shop? Staying means being the one who picks at things, which may change nothing and may get him labelled; leaving is clean and quick, but it amounts to accepting the system, and gives up the feel for the work and the regular customers he has built up.

Pause for ten seconds and decide what you would do.

One possible approach

Assumptions: Assume he has hard data (the past two months of everyone's shifts, output and complaint records), and that the manager is not acting in bad faith, only out of inertia and a dislike of trouble; assume there are two or three other shops nearby he could go to.

What you could do: First turn the shift and output records into one table, listing facts only; ask the manager for a one-to-one talk, about the rules and the measure, not about people; offer a transparent formula for the next split and a transition plan; only if the manager will not even look at the data, talk about staying or going.

Another reasonable choice: Another reasonable choice is simply to leave, for a shop where the split follows written rules. The reason is that once trust collapses, the cost of rebuilding it falls entirely on the individual, and leaving is itself a signal to the shop and to the trade. Both are reasonable; it depends on how he judges whether this shop can still change.

Sources and uncertainty

All the material comes from Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian, in the Hereditary House of Chancellor Chen and the Basic Annals of Xiang Yu, plus Ban Gu's Book of Han, which rewrites the Records (A-E10 shares a source with A-E4); there is no genuinely independent second source. The "taolao feast — coarse fare" scene is highly dramatic and cannot be checked. "Fan Zeng left because of the deception" is the causal account given by the Records (suspicion -> his advice not followed -> asks to leave -> dies of illness on the road), not independent evidence. Fan Zeng's words on leaving differ between the two chapters ("I beg leave to resign and return home" in the Hereditary House of Chancellor Chen; "I beg leave to resign and return to the ranks" in the Basic Annals of Xiang Yu). Chen Ping's stated reasons for leaving Wei and Chu come from his own account to the King of Han and may contain self-justification (the author's inference). "An affair with his sister-in-law" and "taking the generals' money" are only accusations by generals in the Han camp, with no independent verification. The inner thoughts of Xiang Yu and Fan Zeng are not recorded in the source texts and are not written into this article. The conversion of 'the third year of Han' to 204 BC follows the standard chronology and is not taken from the evidence above.
