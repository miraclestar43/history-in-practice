# Turning "Not Acceptable" into a List of Clauses: One Act of Persuasion in the Ili Negotiation

*After Chong Hou concluded a treaty with Russia, the court debated it at length without reaching a decision. Zuo Zongtang's memorial put the objection into items — the border terms and the trade terms, each marked as unacceptable — and set out a two-stage plan of negotiation followed by battle. After the memorial was commended, he volunteered to take command at Hami. Faced with a badly negotiated agreement, opposing it is not enough: you have to say which clauses cannot move, and what stands behind your demand to talk again.*

## The Treaty Was Signed; the Debate Would Not End

Russia had earlier sent troops in and seized Ili, in Xinjiang. Zuo Zongtang, who had taken the western affairs upon himself and led the campaign in Xinjiang, asked that the return of Ili be negotiated with Russia, and the court sent Chong Hou, a plenipotentiary envoy, to Russia, where he concluded a treaty. Chong Hou was censured by court officials, and the debate dragged on toward no conclusion. One reading, the author's, is that the trouble was never whether Ili mattered, but that the treaty had already been signed. Accept it, and the lost territory and compromised rights stood as a fait accompli. Repudiate it, and someone had to go back to the table carrying enough leverage to make the other side sit down again. [QSG4122-E2][QSG4122-E4][QSG4122-E5][QSG4122-E6][QSG4122-E11][QSG4122-E17][QSG4122-E18]

Into that deadlock stepped Zuo Zongtang, who had taken the western affairs upon himself, with a memorial. Item by item he named the terms that must not be accepted, and argued — in the history's words, given here in translation, as are all English quotations in this piece — that one should "first take the matter up in negotiation, tactful and alert to opportunity, then settle it on the field of battle, patient and set on victory." The throne commended it; Chong Hou was punished and dismissed, and the court sent Zeng Jize to Russia to revise the treaty. Zuo then asked leave to move his headquarters to Hami. Afterward, peace was concluded and Ili was returned. [QSG4122-E10][QSG4122-E11][QSG4122-E12][QSG4122-E15][QSG4122-E17]

## Who Held Ili

Start with the situation the memorial landed in. The figure the biography calls the Pasha was a Muslim chieftain from beyond the frontier, originally a heshuo beg — a local chief — of the Andijan people, who, fearing pressure from Russia, crossed into the frontier region. He seized Kashgar and gradually encroached on the southern cities (the biography's "eight southern cities"). He then defeated Tuoming, who held Urumqi, and so took over the northern cities of Ili and collected their revenues. He could command the Muslim population, sent envoys to seek support from Britain and Russia, and bought weapons to arm his own forces. The British backed him quietly, hoping to see him set up a separate state that would stand as a shield against Russia. The history says that at that time, because Muslim forces repeatedly raided its border, Russia sent troops, drove them out, seized Ili, and announced that it would go on to take Urumqi on China's behalf. [QSG4122-E1][QSG4122-E2][QSG4122-E19]

At the time of the Ili crisis, the court ordered Zuo Zongtang to detach troops to garrison Suzhou, and he sent Xu Zhanbiao with six thousand men. The biography records a separate episode: when Russia was at war with Turkey, Jin Shun, one of the commanders under Zuo, asked to exploit the opening and strike Ili. Zuo refused: "No. If our army does not act from a position of right, we hand them their excuse." [QSG4122-E3][QSG4122-E16][QSG4122-E12]

One reading of it is that he would rather let an opening to strike slip by than give the other side a justification to use afterward. As for how he weighed the decision, the material used here gives only this line, and we do not speculate beyond it. [QSG4122-E16]

In the first month of the fourth year, he submitted a plan for turning Xinjiang into a regular province. He also asked the court to negotiate two matters with Russia: the return of Ili, and the handing over of the rebels who had fled into Russia. By then the eastern and western four cities of southern Xinjiang had been recovered in turn. The rebels item relates to an episode recorded earlier in the biography: Bek Quli took in Bai Yanhu, and when the main army arrived he fled into Russia. The court sent Chong Hou, a plenipotentiary, to Russia. [QSG4122-E4][QSG4122-E5][QSG4122-E20]

## Three Sets of Accounts in the Memorial

The Russian side laid out three heads of demand: trade, borders, and indemnity. Chong Hou concluded the treaty, and quickly — the history reaches for one blunt word: "Chong Hou hastily concluded the treaty." Chong Hou was then censured by court officials, and the debate went on without decision. The treaty had been signed, yet Chong Hou had been censured and the debate stalled; the whole affair hung in mid-air. [QSG4122-E5]

Zuo's memorial opened by doing the arithmetic. Since Russia occupied Ili it had been nibbling away without pause, until Xinjiang looked set to shrink by a hundred li a day. Russia treated Ili as an outer treasury: the moment we asked for the land back, it demanded five million rubles in compensation. "So Russia's return of Ili would cost Russia nothing; what we would get is only a wasteland on the outskirts." One reading, the author's, is that the recovery would be nominal, and the real profit would stay on the other side. The figure is recorded as the history gives it and has not been verified. [QSG4122-E6]

Next, the border. Chong Hou had further proposed to hand Russia the country around the Chorgos and Tekes rivers, which amounted to cutting away the land southwest of Ili. The memorial pressed the point: "In an age when our arms are not strong, there have been those who carved up territory to buy peace. Here, without a single arrow loosed, we would cast away a vital place — this is the border term that cannot be accepted." Not one battle fought, and the key ground surrendered first. [QSG4122-E7]

The second item was trade. Russian merchants were after commerce, and the Russian government was planting consulates far and wide, meaning to "use trade to penetrate deep into the interior" — "this is the trade term that cannot be accepted." The border clause is a piece of land you can see; the trade clause is a road running inward. [QSG4122-E8]

The memorial also named the calculation on the other side: "I observe that the Russians harbor ill intent, presuming that our country may be weary of war, and so they use a fully empowered envoy to bind the hands of the frontier commander." They had wrongly presumed us tired of fighting, and turned a plenipotentiary into a leash on the man in the field. [QSG4122-E9]

The course the memorial proposed was: "first take the matter up in negotiation, tactful and alert to opportunity, then settle it on the field of battle, patient and set on victory" — closing with "Though I am old, tired, and of no great ability, how dare I not do my best." Negotiation and battle were not alternatives to choose between; they were two steps in a fixed order. [QSG4122-E10]

The memorial went up. The throne found the words stirring and commended them; Chong Hou was punished and dismissed, and Zeng Jize was sent to Russia to revise the earlier treaty. Thereupon Zuo volunteered to take the field at Hami and plan the recovery of Ili: Jin Shun would advance on Jinghe as the eastern column, Zhang Yao along the Tekes as the central column, and Jintang — Songshan's nephew, who had taken Songshan's place after Songshan died in battle — through the Burut grazing lands as the western column, while Tan Shanglian and others garrisoned Kashgar, Aksu, and Hami in support along the line of communications — more than forty thousand cavalry and infantry all told. [QSG4122-E11][QSG4122-E12][QSG4122-E21]

In the fourth month of the sixth year Zuo set out from Suzhou with a coffin in his carriage, and reached Hami in the fifth. Hearing that the imperial army was moving in force, Russia reinforced Ili and the Nalin River and sent warships cruising off the coast to shake the capital; at the same time Tianjin, Fengtian, and Shandong all raised the alarm. Away from the negotiating table, both sides were moving troops. [QSG4122-E13][QSG4122-E14]

In the seventh month the court summoned Zuo to the capital as an adviser and put Jintang in his place. In the first month of the following year, peace was concluded, Ili was handed back, and the coastal defenses were stood down. Between one memorial and one treaty lay Zeng Jize's mission to Russia to revise the treaty, as well as several troop movements, reinforcements, and alarms — and in the end it all came to rest on paper. [QSG4122-E15][QSG4122-E11]

## How the Persuasion Was Done

The author's reading is that this memorial carried weight through its structure, not merely its stance. It broke the objection into items — one for the border terms, one for trade, each with its own stated reason for refusal. It wrote the plan as a sequence: negotiation first, battle second. That Chong Hou was then punished and dismissed, and Zeng Jize sent to revise the earlier treaty, is on the record. After the memorial was commended, he volunteered to hold Hami, making the second step concrete — a named man in a named place. [QSG4122-E7][QSG4122-E8][QSG4122-E10][QSG4122-E11][QSG4122-E12]

The layer easily missed is money and grain — though this belongs to the earlier western expedition that recovered Xinjiang. At the outset of that expedition, Zuo asked to borrow from abroad; the court allotted five million and directed him to raise a further five million as a foreign loan. The history says that in twenty months beyond the passes, the cities of northern and southern Xinjiang were all recovered — and that it was the abundance of supply transport that accomplished it. One reading is that the earlier campaign had succeeded on the strength of its supply, and that this record stood behind the call to be "patient and set on victory." The material used here says nothing about supply during the Ili standoff, so the two should not be treated as the same period. [QSG4122-E10][QSG4122-E17][QSG4122-E18]

As for success and failure, the history records that peace was concluded and Ili returned. Just before that, it says, in a phrase translated here, that "Russia, too, was awed by our military might and feared an outright rupture," presenting Russia's fear of a rupture as what led to the settlement; this is the history's own judgment, and it has not been checked against other material here. To read this episode as a lesson that the war party was right and the peace party wrong goes beyond what the material can support. [QSG4122-E15]

## How This Works Today

> A procurement renewal lands in your hands, with prices that adjust to the market and a minimum purchase commitment inside it. There are two voices internally: one wants to sign the whole package, the other wants to start over. The usable move is to break the clauses apart: which two are not open to concession, which few can be traded, and what you would take in exchange — written out one line at a time. An objection becomes negotiable only when it is itemized.

> The other thing is to let the preparation be seen. Work up the evaluation of alternative suppliers, and work out the timing and the cost of a switch; put it on the table in internal meetings, and where necessary let the other side see it too. When they know you can genuinely walk away, terms may loosen — or they may not, and that is the price to be paid.

## The Boundaries of the Evidence

This piece rests on a single source, the biography of Zuo Zongtang in the Draft History of the Qing, compiled in 1928; its account may not match the archives of the time. The memorial and the dialogue — such as "first take the matter up in negotiation" and "our army does not act from a position of right" — are the history's rendering and may not be verbatim. Quoted passages in English are the author's translations of the classical Chinese. [QSG4122-E10][QSG4122-E16]

Dates follow the reign years of the original and are not converted to Western years. Figures are copied as given and not checked: five million rubles, more than forty thousand cavalry and infantry, twenty months beyond the passes. The source does record the agenda: the return of Ili and the handing over of the rebels, which Zuo requested, and Russia's three heads of demand, namely trade, borders, and indemnity. The full terms of Chong Hou's treaty and the substance of Zeng Jize's revision lie outside the passages this piece draws on, and this piece does not fill them in. The history presents Russia's being awed and fearing a rupture as leading into the settlement; this is the history's own judgment and has not been checked against other material here. ## Think about it

A cooperation agreement negotiated by a duly authorized party — on plainly unfavorable terms — is on the table. Internally, opinion is split and the discussion has stalled: some say accept it and cut your losses, others say throw it out entirely.

**Would you accept the deal and cut your losses first, or first settle which clauses cannot be conceded, and then show the other side you have a credible alternative and the readiness to bear the cost?**

<details><summary>One possible approach</summary>

- **Assumptions**: Assume the agreement is not yet being carried out and there is still room to renegotiate; assume your side can absorb a stretch of cost; assume the other party cares about the long-term relationship rather than a single one-off advantage.
- **What you could do**: One: break the terms into individual clauses, marking what is unacceptable, what can be conceded, and what can be traded. Two: for every unacceptable clause, prepare an alternative and a cost sheet, and make your capacity to bear that cost real. Three: move in the order of negotiation first and action second, so that the other side can see your preparation.
- **Another reasonable choice**: The other approach, the one the author suggests, is to accept the deal and cut losses while pressing for a shorter term, review points, or reciprocal clauses. This is simply the alternative the author offers.
